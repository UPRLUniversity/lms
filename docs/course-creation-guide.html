Before you startHow to use this document

This template mirrors the actual course-builder screens in the LMS, section for section. Fill it in offline — on paper, in a PDF reader, or in a browser — then either enter it yourself in the builder or hand it to your course administrator to enter for you. Nothing you write here is submitted anywhere automatically.

Work through the parts in order: A → B → C → D → E → F. A course cannot be published until it has at least one module, one lesson, a summary, and a cover image (Part A + C) — everything else (assignments, quizzes, programme placement) can be added later, even after the course is live.

What a course is built from

A course is a single, ongoing offering (there is no term/semester in this LMS — a course stays open across enrolment periods, not tied to a particular session). Its content is organised as:

Course → one or more Modules → each module holds an ordered mix of Lessons, Assignments and Quizzes/Exams in a single sequence (so a quiz can sit between two lessons, or an assignment can stand entirely on its own, outside any module).

Part BProgramme & fee placement

This is what makes the course count toward a qualification and what it costs — a separate question from which department teaches it (Part A). A course can sit in several programmes/parts at once (up to 10); exactly one placement must be marked primary.

There is no manual "set a price" field anywhere in the builder. Price is always derived automatically from the course's primary programme placement: it takes that programme's published per-paper fee. If a course has no primary placement, it is free. Registration and administration fees are charged once per student, per programme — never per course, never discounted.

Part E · optionalQuizzes, exams & question bank

A quiz/exam ("assessment") draws either a fixed list of questions you choose, or a pooled random selection from categories you define. Where you place it in the module sequence decides whether it's a pre-module check, a post-module test, or a standalone exam — this is worked out automatically from its position, not a separate setting.

Course lifecycle (for your information — not something you set directly)

Draft → Review → Published → Archived. A course starts life as Draft the moment Part A is submitted; it moves through Review on the way to Published, which is the only state visible to students in the catalogue.

Appendix 2Worked example — a real course, filled in

This is an actual course from the LMS's demo data, shown here as a model of how much detail to aim for in each field.

Part A — Course basics Title: Foundations of Public Relations  ·  Code: PRL101  ·  Level: Undergraduate  ·  Department: Department of Public Relations (Faculty of Communication & Media Studies)

Summary: "Master the principles, history and practice of modern public relations in a Nigerian context."

Description: "An introduction to the discipline of public relations: its theories, publics, tools and ethics. You will build the vocabulary and core skills every PR practitioner needs."

Learning objectives: Define public relations and its role in organisations · Identify and segment publics · Draft a basic press release · Apply the RACE planning model
Part C — Curriculum (shape, not full text) 3 modules, 2–3 lessons each, 6–20 minutes per lesson. Mix of types: a YouTube video (the first one marked as a free preview so visitors can sample the course), rich-text lessons, and one external-link lesson. Total course runs a little over an hour of content.
Part D — Assignments "Crisis communication case study" — attached to a module, hand in File or Text, due in 7 days, late submissions not allowed, graded out of 30 against the "PR writing rubric" (Message clarity / Audience fit / Craft & accuracy — each Excellent / Good / Needs work), required to complete the course.

"Personal reflection brief" — standalone (not tied to a module), hand in Text, due date already passed but late submissions allowed, graded out of 20, not required, not rubric-graded.
Part E — Quizzes Three question categories (Fundamentals / Application / Advanced) holding ~30 questions across all seven question types. A pre-module quiz and a post-module quiz pair (passing score 50%), a timed 20-minute standalone "Final exam" (2 attempts allowed, questions shuffled, answers shown immediately after each attempt), a pooled "Practice pool" quiz, and an essay-only "Reflection essay" quiz marked by hand.

Appendix 3What the platform cannot do yet

Worth knowing before you plan around them — these are honest current gaps, not settings you missed.

There is no term/semester/academic-session concept. A course is evergreen: it opens for enrolment and stays open according to its own enrolment window (Part A), not a shared academic calendar.
There is no "prerequisite course" field. Sequencing within a course is possible (Progression mode → Sequential, Part A), but the LMS cannot yet require "Course X before Course Y."
Co-instructors cannot be added through the product yet. Whoever creates the course becomes its sole lead instructor; adding a second instructor currently requires a database change by an engineer, not a self-serve step. Plan single-instructor courses for now.
There is no manual per-course price field in any form. Price is 100% derived from programme placement (Part B) — if you need a one-off discount or a free promotional course outside programme pricing, that has to be handled outside this template, by an administrator.
None of the above block you from building a complete, publishable course today — they're just edges to route around (e.g. put prerequisite context in the description, or note "recommended order" in module titles) until the corresponding feature ships.
